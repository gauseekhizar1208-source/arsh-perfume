ARSH PERFUME — PROJECT DOCUMENTATIONIntroduction

1. INTRODUCTION

ARSH PERFUME is a web-based e-commerce application for presenting and selling attars, roll-on perfumes and fragrance oils. The application provides a branded customer storefront and a protected administration panel within one Laravel project. It supports product discovery, variant-level pricing and stock, shopping carts, checkout, payments, order fulfilment and customer account management.

The website is designed for Indian delivery addresses and INR transactions. Customers can browse the catalogue without an account, while registered customers gain a wishlist, saved addresses and order history. The administrator manages the catalogue, stock, orders, content and business settings from a centralized workspace.

1.1 Existing System

The comparison baseline is a conventional small-store workflow in which product information is shared over messages or a basic catalogue, orders are recorded in spreadsheets, and payment and dispatch updates are checked manually. This baseline explains the problem addressed by the application; it is not a claim that a formal field survey of the store was conducted.

  1. Customer asks for available fragrances, sizes and prices.
  2. Store staff check stock and communicate availability.
  3. Order and address details are recorded manually.
  4. Payment is checked separately and the parcel is dispatched.
  5. Stock sheets and customer updates are maintained independently.

Limitations of the comparison workflow

  • Repeated manual communication for product information and order status.
  • Separate price, stock and order records can become inconsistent.
  • Historical sales and customer information are difficult to consolidate.
  • Payment references and cancellations require careful reconciliation.

1.2 Need for the New System

A single online platform is needed to present accurate product information, preserve purchase records and coordinate the relationship between customer orders, payments and stock. A customer should be able to discover a fragrance, choose an available size and place an order without depending on repeated manual messages.

For the store owner, the system should provide one place to maintain prices, stock, promotional content, order status and reporting. Payment states and fulfilment states must remain distinct so that a customer-entered transaction reference or a browser redirect cannot be mistaken for confirmed receipt of money.

1.3 Objective of the New System

  • Provide a searchable, responsive catalogue with categories, fragrance filters and product details.
  • Maintain price, sale price, availability and low-stock thresholds for each product variant.
  • Support guest and signed-in carts, coupons and validated checkout.
  • Calculate totals on the server using shared shipping, discount and tax rules.
  • Support COD, verified Razorpay payments and direct UPI with manual bank-receipt verification.
  • Reserve stock during order placement and restore cancelled reservations once.
  • Allow customers to manage their accounts, addresses, wishlist and own orders.
  • Provide an administrator workspace for products, inventory, customers, content and reports.
  • Maintain order history, stock movements and an administrator activity trail.
  • Document configuration, testing, known limitations and operation for future maintenance.

1.4 Problem Definition

Develop a maintainable e-commerce website for ARSH PERFUME that connects product browsing, variant selection, cart management, validated checkout, payment handling, stock reservation and order fulfilment. The application must prevent unauthorized access to customer records, avoid trusting browser-submitted totals and preserve the purchased item and address details as historical snapshots.

The scope includes a public storefront, customer account area and administrator panel. The source does not implement a marketplace for multiple sellers, a machine-learning recommendation service or a separate mobile application.

1.8 Advantages and Limitations

Advantages

  • A centralized product catalogue and administration workspace reduce repeated data entry.
  • Variant-level pricing and stock support different fragrance sizes.
  • Server-side total calculation keeps cart, checkout and order amounts consistent.
  • Transactional placement and row locking protect stock during competing checkouts.
  • Item, address and receiving-account snapshots preserve purchase-time information.
  • Signed payment verification and duplicate-event handling support payment integrity.
  • Role checks and ownership policies protect administration and customer records.
  • Reports, history and movement logs support operational review.
  • Responsive layouts support desktop and mobile browser use.

Current limitations

  • Direct UPI and UPI refunds involve external bank verification or transfer.
  • CSV product export works; the product-import route lacks its controller implementation.
  • Category ordering uses numeric sort values rather than drag-and-drop interaction.
  • SMS/WhatsApp notifications, multiple currencies and multilingual content are not implemented.
  • The automated suite currently contains two existing failures, described in Section 5.7.
  • A live gateway transaction and a real Google consent round trip require configured credentials and separate verification.
  • No measured production-load or uptime guarantee is established by this report.
This report describes the implemented source and observed checks. Proposed enhancements and planning allocations are clearly distinguished from verified functionality.

5. DEVELOPMENT

5.1 Development Approach

The implementation uses a layered, server-rendered Laravel architecture. Controllers coordinate requests, Form Requests validate structured input, services implement business operations, Eloquent models represent persistence, and Blade views render the interface. Browser libraries add local interactions, motion, QR generation and dashboard charts.

The repository demonstrates modular implementation and automated checks. The available evidence does not establish a historical sprint cadence, daily meetings or individual commit contributions. The staged process below is a practical description of how the modules are organized and should be maintained.

  1. Identify users, product information, payment methods and order rules.
  2. Design the database and relationships through versioned migrations.
  3. Implement public discovery, authentication and customer account screens.
  4. Implement shared cart, pricing, coupon, order and inventory services.
  5. Add verified payment flows, administration, content and reports.
  6. Exercise isolated tests and preview screens; record gaps before release.

Important implementation decisions

  • Prices and stock belong to product variants rather than only the parent product.
  • Checkout runs within a transaction, locks variants in stable order and revalidates current price/stock.
  • Order placement reserves stock immediately and records purchase-time snapshots.
  • Payment and fulfilment states are independent.
  • Runtime business settings use the settings table; secrets remain in server environment configuration.

Source: app/Http/; app/Services/; app/Models/; resources/views/.

Validation and error handling

Use CheckoutRequest and ProductRequest for structured validation, with controller/service checks for method availability, ownership and state transitions. Authentication and sensitive data

Passwords use Laravel hashing rather than plain text or a generic SHA-256 password scheme. Google OAuth linking requires the explicit account flow; matching an existing email alone does not silently link accounts. Uploaded raster images are type/size/dimension validated and stored under random names.

Money and transactions

Use Money conversions and PricingService instead of duplicating floating-point total calculations in templates or controllers. Apply inventory mutations through InventoryService and retain order status and stock movement history.

5.5 Implementation Details (continued)

Authentication and customer access

Email registration/login, password reset and optional Google sign-in are implemented through dedicated controllers. My Account supports profile, password and address changes. Ownership policies protect customer order and address actions. Disabled users are signed out by active-account middleware.

Checkout, payments and stock

OrderService rechecks stock and prices, locks variants in stable ID order, validates coupons, saves purchase snapshots and reserves stock in a transaction. COD collection is recorded separately. Razorpay uses server totals, signed callbacks/webhooks and duplicate-event handling. UPI references remain unverified until an administrator checks actual receipt.

Fulfilment and content

The configured order lifecycle is pending → confirmed → processing → shipped → out for delivery → delivered, with permitted cancellation and return branches. Administrators can add tracking information, view invoices and record refunds. Pages, FAQs, banners and homepage settings provide content management.

7. CONCLUSION

ARSH PERFUME brings fragrance discovery, shopping, order processing and store administration into one web application. The implementation includes a responsive storefront, product variants, carts, coupons, customer accounts, multiple payment paths, inventory tracking, fulfilment tools and owner-managed content.

The principal engineering contribution is the connection between order integrity and day-to-day retail operations: totals are calculated on the server, stock is reserved transactionally, purchase details are snapshotted, ownership is checked and payment confirmation is kept separate from customer-entered claims. The administration panel adds the operational tools required to maintain this information.

Technical learning and challenges represented by the project

  • Separating request handling, reusable business rules and persistence.
  • Handling simultaneous stock changes and repeated order/payment events.
  • Protecting customer data while supporting both guests and registered buyers.
  • Integrating external identity/payment services without trusting browser-only outcomes.
  • Building responsive customer and administration views with a shared backend.
  • Using tests and documentation to expose unresolved environment or behavior gaps.

Current submission status

The source, diagrams, schema dictionary, screenshots and setup/operation instructions are documented in the format of the supplied UDP reference. The known two test failures and incomplete CSV import remain explicit. Real configured provider flows and deployment operation require separate verification before a production-readiness claim can be made.

The project provides a maintainable foundation for an online fragrance store. Future work should first strengthen verification and reconciliation, then add capabilities that are justified by customer and store-owner needs.
